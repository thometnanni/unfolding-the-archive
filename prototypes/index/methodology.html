Unfolding the Archive

Iterative design thinking sessions between researcher and information design / development team, based on the results of desk research and interviews conducted in the first phase of the project. Main points of focus: De-centering the query approach A non-hierarchical approach might be key to making the design process more accessible for various users of architecture archives. Allowing users to see the contents of the archive in a non-hierarchical manner allows to build different sets of relations. (removing folder structure and query) Contextual access Interface is a place of orientation, shows archives as networked and relational. Objects and archives are never isolated. Decontextualisation of born digital archives is one of the biggest risks identified by this research. Allowing multiple readings / interpretations The prototype should consider different users and their digital literacy, it should make technical knowledge more accessible and easier to read and interpret. it should enable users to formulate questions even if they do not have specialised knowledge. Display complexity of the archive, shows design process and its complexity for example by using multimodal forms of access and surfacing the multiplicity of actors and voices in the archive. The use of multimodality can be a strategy to overcome the challenge related to the fact that not all records can be rendered. The challenge in archiving the design process lies in devising a schema that creates relations between objects, which can represent the process in a meaningful way. Striving to surface richness of the record, not only focusing on visual representation, treating records as data - depending on the type of record different types of richness will be surfaced. Data driven interface - flexibility what information to surface, based on the data model, the minimum unit is the record, not a batch. It should be possible to compute over the records.
